# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue with the work, not the person.

This is a small project. It does not need a governance apparatus; it needs people who can disagree
about a specification without it becoming unpleasant.

## What is expected

- **Criticise the claim, not the claimant.** "This inverts the fail-safe and here is the case that
  breaks" is the useful form. "This is amateur work" is not.
- **Say what you actually measured.** The project's whole discipline is separating what you proved
  from what you assumed. That applies to conversations too.
- **Accept a settled question as settled.** A refusal recorded with its reasoning is an answer.
  Re-raising it needs new information, not more insistence.
- **Assume the other person read less context than you, not less carefully.**

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning comments, or unwelcome attention. Publishing someone's
private information. Sustained disruption of a discussion. Any of the above in private channels
after meeting here counts the same.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, and any space where someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report to the maintainer through
[GitHub's private reporting](https://github.com/Solmex72/parvis-protocol/security/advisories/new),
which does not require disclosing anything publicly.

Responses, in rough order: a private word, a public correction, temporary exclusion from a thread,
and a permanent ban. The maintainer decides, explains the decision to the person affected, and
does not litigate it in public — a moderation thread is worse for everyone than a moderation
decision.

I will not pretend this is a neutral process. It is one person's judgement, applied in good faith,
and stated plainly rather than dressed up as procedure.

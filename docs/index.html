The fleet as a warehouse floor — a demo you can break

Agents are cranes. Directories are pallets. Work arrives at an induct and leaves by a spur. An external service is a truck — and a truck docks at the boundary and never drives onto the floor, which is the airlock drawn where you can see it. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, click anything. Then trip the E-stop and watch the floor refuse.

The real thing reads all of this from disk: cranes from session markers in _os/exchange/bus/session/, pallets from the tree, inducts from REQ rows, spurs from the surface directory. Here it is a simulation, which is why this page says so in three places. A page with no live source that presents itself as live is the failure the protocol names first.

Three things to try

Trip the E-stop

The floor goes red, every crane stops, and the induct button refuses. A red floor takes no orders — and red outranks every other colour in the glance.

Click a truck

It has no controls, deliberately. A truck drops a load at a dock and that is the whole of its access. That is the airlock, drawn as a loading bay.

Open a pallet

You are inside another whole warehouse, navigated identically. Every level is a floor, all the way down — same controls, nothing new to learn.

Renderer self-test

The demo doubles as a smoke test of the invariants in protocol/09-FLOOR.md. It runs on load; add ?selftest=1 to the URL to see it run in isolation.
